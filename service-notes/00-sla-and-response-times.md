# SLA & Response Time Options

**Purpose:** Internal notes on possible service-level commitments for managed services (monitoring, DNS security, bundle). These are drafts for review — not yet published.

---

## Context

Shepherd Networks is a side project alongside a full-time 9–5 job. Response commitments need to be honest and achievable. A lunch break can be used for urgent issues when needed, and evenings/weekends are available for scheduled work and non-critical responses.

---

## Proposed Response Times

### Monitoring Alerts (Network Outages, Device Failures)

| Severity | Response Time | Description |
|----------|---------------|-------------|
| Critical | Within 4 business hours | Complete network outage or primary router/gateway failure. I'll acknowledge and begin remote troubleshooting within 4 hours during business days (M–F, 8 AM – 6 PM). |
| High | Within 8 business hours | Single device down (AP, switch, camera) affecting partial connectivity. Acknowledged same business day. |
| Low | Within 1 business day | Performance degradation, intermittent issues, or informational alerts. Reviewed and responded to within the next business day. |

**"Business hours"** = Monday–Friday, 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM Central. The extended window (vs. a strict 9–5) accounts for lunch-hour and end-of-day availability.

**After-hours/weekends:** Alerts are still captured and logged. I review them as time allows, but no guaranteed response time outside business hours. If something critical comes in, I'll make a best effort to respond the same evening or next morning.

### DNS Security Issues

- Configuration changes (allowlist/blocklist updates): within 1 business day
- Reported false positives (legitimate site blocked): within 4 business hours
- Security incidents (active phishing/malware campaign): best-effort same-day response

### Remote Support (Bundle Clients)

- Support requests are scheduled, not on-demand
- Typical turnaround: within 2 business days of request
- Sessions scheduled during lunch, evenings, or weekends by mutual availability
- 1-hour monthly allotment does not roll over

---

## On-Site Work Scheduling

- On-site visits are by appointment only
- Typically scheduled for evenings (after 5:30 PM) or weekends
- Larger projects (installations, site surveys) can be scheduled for days off or PTO days
- Emergency on-site is available but not guaranteed — I'll do my best to accommodate

---

## What This Means for the Website

Options for how to present this:

### Option A: Keep it simple (recommended)
Don't publish specific SLA numbers. Instead, set expectations with language like:
> "I monitor your network around the clock and respond to issues promptly during business hours. Critical alerts are prioritized and addressed the same business day whenever possible."

### Option B: Publish general commitments
Add a brief "What to Expect" section:
> - Critical alerts acknowledged within 4 business hours (M–F)
> - Support requests responded to within 1–2 business days
> - On-site work scheduled by appointment, typically evenings and weekends

### Option C: Include in a formal service agreement
Keep SLA details out of the website entirely and include them in a service agreement or welcome email sent to new clients. This gives flexibility to adjust terms per client.

---

## Recommendation

**Go with Option A for the website** — keep the language warm and confident without boxing yourself into specific numbers publicly. Use Option B or C in direct client communications or a simple service agreement.

The key message: "I take this seriously, I'm responsive, and I'll be upfront about timelines."
